Free chat rooms you join in the browser

A Y-Chat room is a public conversation. Whoever is in the room can read the lines while they are there. You do not download a chat app, and you do not need an account. Come in as a guest, pick a room, and talk.

These rooms are the shared half of Y-Chat. The other half is a private messenger for one person at a time. A line you type in a room is for the room. A line you type in the messenger is not posted there.

How a chat room works

The room list is the first thing you see after you come in. Categories sit along the side on a wide screen, and across the top of the list on a phone. Choose Friends, Music, Sports, Tech, or Random, or leave the filter on All.

Open a room and the conversation takes the screen. The composer stays at the bottom. On a phone it sits above the Rooms, Chat, and People tabs. Send a line and everyone currently in the room sees it. A person who walks in later sees that you are there, and they see new lines from that moment on. They do not get a scrollback of what was said before they arrived.

Leaving is the Rooms tab, or All rooms. That drops you back to the list. The room keeps going for anyone who stayed. Your earlier lines are not filed anywhere.

Messages are short on purpose. A single line can be up to 500 characters. If you send a burst of them too quickly, the room asks you to slow down. The point is a conversation, not a paste of a whole page.

Friends

Welcome Lobby

This is the front door, filed under Friends. The topic is simple: say hi. LobbyBot is always listed here. The first time you enter, it greets you by name. Say hi, ask for help, or send ping, and it answers in the room. A later visit in the same stay does not get a second greeting, so the lobby does not fill with the same hello every time the connection blinks.

LobbyBot can also take a private message. If it does not understand the line, it says it mostly hangs out in the lobby. It is not a person, and you cannot take its name.

20s Lounge

A Friends room for the stretch of life that is past homework and not yet fond of the phrase back pain. The topic is a joke and an age bracket, not a rule. Anyone in the chat can open it.

Late Night

The room for people who are still awake. It stays quiet until someone is. Like every other room, it does not keep the night's conversation for the morning.

Music

Now Playing

A music chat for whatever is on right now. Drop the song, the album, or the thing you turned off. The room does not play audio. It is text, the same as the rest of Y-Chat.

Throwbacks

Songs that still hit. A place in the music category for older tracks, one-hit wonders, and the chorus you still know by heart.

Sports

Game Day

Scores, gripes, and glory. A sports chat for the match that is on, the call that was wrong, and the result you want to tell someone about. Nothing in the room is an official score feed. People type what they see.

Fantasy Desk

Questionable lineup advice, which is the honest description. Bring a roster question. Treat every answer as another person in a chat room, not as a scout.

Tech

Gadgets

Hardware and new toys. Phones, headphones, the thing you just unboxed, the thing you should not have bought. It is a tech chat, still just text.

Code & Coffee

What are you building? A room for side projects, bugs, and the drink next to the keyboard. It is not a support desk and it does not run your code.

Random

Anything Goes

No set topic. The only hint on the door is to try to behave. It is still a public room, so what you type is visible to the people inside it.

Would You Rather

Ask one. Answer one. A random room with a built-in game: pose a choice, then answer someone else's. Good when the other rooms are quiet and you still want a chat.

Start your own room

The list has a way to start a room. Give it a name of at least two characters, choose one of the five categories, and add a topic up to a short sentence. If you leave the topic blank, the room invites people to come say hi. You join the new room as soon as it exists.

Names are unique. A second room cannot reuse a name that is already on the list, even if the letters differ only by case. The directory holds up to 40 rooms, including the ones that were already here. When it is full, the chat says so instead of quietly dropping your room.

A room you create lives in the memory of the computer that is running Y-Chat. It is not written into a history file. Restarting that computer clears rooms that were added on top of the original list, along with everyone who was online. The original rooms come back. The conversations do not, because they were never saved.

Who can see a room?

Public means public to the people in it. There is no secret room and no password on a door. If you need a conversation that the room cannot read, use the private messenger instead of whispering in the chat.

Ignore is personal. If someone's lines are a problem, ignore them and their room messages stop landing for you. They are still in the room for everyone else. There is not a separate team of moderators inside the product. The rooms are only as calm as the people in them.

Chat rooms on a phone

A narrow screen shows one thing at a time. The Rooms tab is the directory. The Chat tab is the room you are in. If you open Chat before you have picked a room, Y-Chat asks you to pick one first. The People tab is the messenger list, not the room.

The composer is pinned so the keyboard does not cover Send. The room list keeps a margin so the last room is not hidden behind the tab bar. A wide screen drops the tabs and shows the directory, the room, and the people together.

What the room does not keep

There is no chat history to search later, and no log you can email to yourself. Refresh the page and the lines disappear. You are placed back in the same room if it still exists, with an empty conversation, so you do not have to find the door again. A brief disconnect, where the page never actually reloaded, keeps the lines you were looking at and reconnects.

That is the trade. A chat room here is easy to enter and impossible to scroll back through tomorrow. Say the thing to the people who are in the room now.
